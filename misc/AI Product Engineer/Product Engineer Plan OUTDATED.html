The professional bet

Judge the work you delegate

Follow one workflow from the user's problem through design, implementation, verification, and observed use. Build the software-engineering judgment to explain those decisions yourself, then use AI to extend what you can accomplish. Change course when the evidence contradicts your assumptions.

Capability

Explain, implement, diagnose, and adapt the central mechanism yourself. Agents can accelerate the surrounding work, but cannot perform the skill being assessed on your behalf.

Product responsibility

Ask an actual operator, customer, colleague, or maintainer what changed in their work. Check usefulness separately from functional correctness.

Working with AI

Study the sixteen mechanisms drawn from Poteto, Kun Chen, Jeffrey Emanuel, and Steve Yegge. Reproduce a bounded example of each and keep the practices that improve your work.

Career options

Choose a career objective that another person or process can confirm, and seek feedback before the final month. Track learning, product results, and market response separately. Completing the plan does not guarantee employment.

The core is senior-level product development with AI. Practice critical use of coding agents in every module; use the dedicated applied-AI module to build a model-backed product capability. Model-training surveys and large personal agent-platform projects are outside this time budget. A title, tool, salary premium, or forecast provides no guaranteed advantage.

Budget 468 hours across 52 learning weeks of nine hours. Study, recruitment, coding, setup, failed attempts, external reviews, and career work all fit inside that allocation. With permission, complete deliberate exercises during your job and count the time once; ordinary job output is not an extra assignment. Interrupted weeks move the completion date without creating catch-up debt.
What the research supports

DORA reports an association between greater AI adoption, higher delivery throughput, and greater instability. The association does not establish that AI causes either outcome in every team. Assess the delivery system as a whole rather than treating faster code generation as sufficient evidence of improvement. [O16]

In a small randomized study, 52 mostly junior developers learning an unfamiliar Python library scored lower on an immediate quiz when using AI assistance. Qualitative analysis associated concept-focused interaction with better learning, but did not establish causation. The study neither measures long-term effects in senior engineers nor validates this schedule. [L1]

Use that uncertainty to guide the curriculum: preserve independent reasoning, test actual behavior, learn the domain, and seek other people's judgment. Reassess those choices as the evidence changes. They do not require a prediction that every software role will evolve in the same way.

Learning and assessment

Test what you can explain, apply, and transfer

Study → Predict → Attempt → Use AI → Challenge → Transfer

Learning mode

Write your hypothesis, caller sketch, failure interleaving, or core implementation before requesting a solution. Use the source, documentation, compiler, and debugger yourself. A first attempt of fifteen to twenty-five minutes is a useful default; seek a hint when needed. After a hint, close the answer and reconstruct the mechanism.

Delivery mode

Once you can state the scope, invariants, permissions, and acceptance evidence, delegate broad or repetitive edits. Inspect consequential changes and run the actual path. Return to learning mode whenever you cannot explain a proposed abstraction or failure. Spend independent effort on that mechanism rather than hand-typing boilerplate.

04 / The four practitioner field guides

Learn sixteen practices from four case studies

Read each practitioner's account, inspect the mechanism, and try the corresponding exercise. Keep the source's practice distinct from the curriculum's adaptation. All sixteen lessons recur in the weekly track so you can revisit structure, evidence, authority, and continuity as the tools change.

Poteto

Poteto: enforce constraints and test the running product

Pstack combines architectural constraints with task-specific procedures and tests of the actual application. Its verification generator starts with the repository's existing ways to run, control, observe, and isolate the product. Its architect skill uses structural sketches before implementation. [P1] [P2] [P4]

P1 Constraints before reminders

Foundation: Information hiding, domain modeling, and type safety.

Choose a repeated misuse and write an invalid example first. Change the constructor, API, or dependency boundary so it rejects or contains that misuse. Then ask an agent with no prior task context to extend the design.

Explain which invariant the design enforces and which still needs a runtime check. Judge complexity separately from line count.

P2 Build an observable feedback loop

Foundation: Testability, debugging interfaces, and behavioral oracles.

Start with the existing test runner, HTTP harness, or browser harness. Add a control or observation only where one is missing. Run success and failure paths from a clean state, then inspect the relevant persisted result.

Show a defect the harness detects and one it cannot. A screenshot cannot establish authorization, durability, or the absence of races.

P3 Use the right kind of parallel reasoning

Foundation: Design alternatives, experiment design, and skeptical review.

Try two alternative sketches for a consequential boundary. Use parallel workers for separate verification slices and a fresh reviewer for a finished diff. Hold the requirement constant and compare the findings.

Explain the different purposes of alternatives, coverage, and critique. Two models agreeing does not supply two independent proofs.

P4 Garden continuously, and prune the rules

Foundation: Refactoring, architectural fitness functions, and maintenance economics.

Sample recent changes and fix a recurring problem at its cause. Add a narrow check if it prevents recurrence, then test that check against a legitimate case. Delete a stale instruction or unnecessary wrapper.

Explain the cost of false positives and when you would revisit the rule. Retain design rationale that the code cannot express.

Apply pstack selectively while keeping your judgment and learning explicit. Cloud infrastructure is optional. Treat reported PR volume as a description of the author's output, not a productivity guarantee.

Kun Chen

Kun Chen: define worker permissions and supervision

Firstmate separates the agent you talk to from workers and persistent supervisors. It uses event-driven supervision and durable task state. No-mistakes validates changes in isolation and distinguishes mechanical repairs from changes to intent. The quota-axi utility provides quota observations for routing. [C1] [C2] [C3] [C4]

C1 Separate investigation from authorization to ship

Foundation: Requirements analysis, delegation, and decision rights.

Write a read-only investigation brief and an implementation brief for the same issue. Specify the unknowns, allowed files and tools, stopping rule, expected output, and publication authority.

Identify an answer that is technically competent but solves the wrong problem. Escalate consequential uncertainty even when the proposed action is reversible.

C2 Isolate changes and bind approval to evidence

Foundation: Git, reproducible builds, capability boundaries, and integration.

Use a separate branch, worktree, or isolated environment. Record the reviewed revision, checks, and artifact. Change the revision after approval and show why the earlier verdict no longer covers it.

Explain the boundary of the isolation. Shared databases, credentials, and deployment targets can remain exposed, and isolated changes can still conflict semantically.

C3 Choose models by task requirements

Foundation: Cost modeling, workload classification, and calibration.

Compare a fast eligible model with a stronger model on two ordinary tasks. Record the outcome, your review time, elapsed time, and spend. Add a routing rule only when the difference is useful.

Separate capability requirements from quota availability. Exhausted quota must not silently lower the required security or reasoning standard.

C4 Keep the supervisor’s context and authority small

Foundation: Control-plane design, observability, and failure detection.

Have a liaison summarize two isolated tasks, then interrupt a worker. Require an explicit owner, current state, and evidence before restarting. Local processes are enough for this exercise.

Explain why an unreachable worker may still be running. Identify when direct human supervision is simpler than adding a manager.

Begin with one agent and independent review where needed. Try a firstmate in a bounded exercise; retaining it, adding secondmates, or using remote hardware depends on the work.

Jeffrey Emanuel

Jeffrey Emanuel: make tasks independent of the conversation

The Flywheel separates global planning from self-contained execution tasks, with durable coordination and memory supporting both. Beads Rust stores work records and dependencies. Beads Viewer analyzes the graph to help select work. The exercises use these mechanisms without requiring the full installation. [E1] [E3] [E4]

E1 Make uncertainty legible before delegating

Foundation: Specification, tradeoff analysis, and empirical discovery.

Describe the user, constraints, invariants, unknowns, and acceptance evidence in a short behavior model. Request a competing plan. Decide which uncertainty needs a prototype rather than further planning.

Explain when a stable compatibility target warrants detailed planning and when a hypothesis about users requires observation instead.

E2 Create restartable work packets

Foundation: Task decomposition, dependency graphs, and interface design.

Split a real change into four to eight tasks. Give each packet its purpose, prerequisites, affected areas, tests, and completion evidence. Hand one to a fresh worker without the preceding chat.

Find a missing dependency and one that needlessly serializes work. Shared invariants, rather than file size, determine whether tasks are independent.

E3 Keep context current and verifiable

Foundation: Documentation, source reliability, cache invalidation, and knowledge retrieval.

Write a short current-state note with links to code and commits, unresolved questions, and an owner. Resume in a fresh session. Include one deliberately stale claim and require the worker to check it against the source.

Distinguish current authoritative state, historical rationale, and unverified memory. One successful workaround is not enough to justify a universal rule.

E4 Turn repeated friction into a small tool

Foundation: Automation, API design, and feedback economics.

Choose a repeated inspection or transformation. Build a small rerunnable command with clear inputs, useful errors, and safe cleanup. Compare it once with the manual procedure.

Explain whether the command saves work after accounting for maintenance. Retain the reusable operation rather than expanding it into a platform.

Detail upcoming work as needed and isolate changes. Graph analysis helps schedule tasks; it cannot establish product priorities. Assess correctness independently of plan length, bead count, or the amount of recorded evidence.

Steve Yegge

Steve Yegge: preserve responsibility across sessions

Yegge distinguishes a durable seat from a temporary session and describes persistent roles supported by deterministic monitors. The monitors invoke models when judgment is needed. Use his handoff practices to study lifecycle management without adopting his forecasts or claims about model welfare as premises. [Y1] [Y2]

Y1 Separate a role from a process

Foundation: Service identity, lifecycle management, and technical ownership.

Define a bounded operational role through its purpose, state, permissions, human owner, and escalation path. Start with release-note drafting or read-only triage. Run the role in a fresh session using only its durable record.

Explain what must survive a restart and what should expire. Verify the new session's work even when it resumes an established identity.

Y2 Automate observation; reserve judgment for ambiguity

Foundation: Monitoring, event processing, and system boundaries.

Write a deterministic check that emits a compact event. Invoke an agent to interpret an ambiguous result or propose a response. Authorize any write separately.

Demonstrate that idle monitoring makes no model calls. Explain how false alarms, duplicate events, and hostile log text can affect the response.

Y3 Make handoff and recovery explicit

Foundation: Graceful shutdown, cancellation, leases, and fault handling.

Interrupt a bounded job after partial progress. Record completed work, the current revision, pending effects, and the next safe action. Resume without repeating the effect or erasing another owner's work.

Identify ambiguous state explicitly. Explain why a timeout reports an observation rather than proving cancellation.

Y4 Feed operational outcomes into better engineering

Foundation: Postmortems, backpressure, deployment safety, and organizational learning.

Inject a recoverable failure or integration conflict. Trace it, choose rollback or repair, and change one structural safeguard. Limit incoming work when validation cannot keep up.

Distinguish a local defect from incentives that encourage it. Preserve the incident record and make a new operating rule only when the cause warrants one.

Use one bounded role or a local simulation. Keep main healthy and production actions authorized. Do not assume unlimited tokens or build a standing agent organization by default.

Use the weekly career allocation

Pool the 30-minute weekly career allocation when useful. Hold one substantive conversation each month with a practitioner, sponsor, maintainer, or hiring contact. Share concise evidence when there is something worth challenging, and check role requirements quarterly. An architectural or product review can serve both purposes; count its time once. Public monthly posts and full quarterly interview loops remain optional.

At weeks 13, 26, 39, and 52, inspect roughly eight to twelve relevant first-party postings or internal role expectations. Filter for location, level, work authorization, and desired responsibilities. Deduplicate employers and distinguish roles. Look for repeated requirements, requested evidence, and one actual skill gap. Use the sample to choose your next action, not to infer a labor-market trend. Consult the research library for a specific unresolved question.

Keep one short case outline: problem → rejected alternative → consequential design → verification → observed outcome → limitations. Spend a few minutes updating it monthly, then seek critique. Use approved summaries or sanitized reproductions for private employer work. Never export protected code, customer data, internal traces, or secrets for a public portfolio.

Use the externally checkable goal to choose a direction, not as a guarantee or sole measure of success. If the outcome does not materialize, examine access, demonstrated capability, evidence quality, role fit, hiring conditions, and outreach. Determine the missing condition before prescribing more study or more agents.
