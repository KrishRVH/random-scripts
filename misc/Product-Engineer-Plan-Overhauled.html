Become the engineer who owns the proof.

You already know how to build software. Spend the next year becoming harder to commoditize: decide what matters, shape codebases agents cannot easily degrade, build executable verification, direct heterogeneous agents safely, operate the result, and remain accountable for the user and business outcome.

The plan does not train “prompt engineering.” It trains the scarce work around cheap code generation: judgment, constraints, verification, orchestration, and consequence.

The durable professional identity

Own the decisions and constraints around the code

Implementation remains necessary, but it is becoming cheaper and more abundant. Your moat is the compound ability to choose, shape, constrain, verify, operate, and explain the result.

Moat 01

Product judgment

Find consequential problems, reject weak ideas, shape scope, and tie work to changed behavior.

Moat 02

Domain leverage

Know the money, risk, policy, workflow, and failure modes that a generic model cannot infer from a ticket.

Moat 03

Architecture

Encode intent in types, APIs, modules, state machines, lints, and migration paths so the safe path is easiest.

Moat 04

Verification

Give agents tools to control the real product, collect evidence, run evals, and expose false confidence.

Moat 05

Agent operations

Route work, isolate authority, supervise exceptions, recover state, measure economics, and own the outcome.

Marketability=outcomes×technical judgment×verification×domain depth×visible evidence
This is multiplicative, not additive. A dazzling agent demo with no user outcome, a product case study with no technical depth, or expert code with no inspectable evidence is easy to discount.
Market thesis · researched September 2026

Prepare for compression, not extinction

The safest working hypothesis is that fewer people will be paid merely to translate tickets into code, while demand persists for people who can deploy AI into real systems, control risk, and own ambiguous outcomes. Re-test the hypothesis every quarter.

Signal 01 · Demand

Software work is still growing

The U.S. BLS projects 10% growth for software developers, QA analysts, and testers from 2025–35, with about 106,100 openings per year. AI and security are cited as demand drivers.

Signal 02 · Baseline shift

AI literacy is becoming normal

Indeed reported that software-development postings mentioned AI at least 20% of the time by December 2025, even amid broader hiring weakness. “Can use AI” will not remain a differentiator by itself.

Signal 03 · Amplification

The delivery system decides the gain

DORA’s 2025 research frames AI as an amplifier: strong sociotechnical systems compound the benefit; weak systems amplify instability and waste.

Signal 04 · Skill mix

Judgment and security rise with AI

Employer research continues to emphasize AI/data, cybersecurity, analytical and creative thinking, adaptability, and leadership. The defensible profile combines technical and human systems skills.

Four AI-engineering schools · distilled

Take the mechanisms; reject the theater

These approaches solve different bottlenecks. Train them in priority order and only after the preceding layer works.

Lauren “poteto” Tan · garden / laboratory

Make quality elastic

foundation · highest

The codebase and verifier should teach, constrain, and prove the work so contributor count can rise without quality collapsing.

Adopt

  • Project-local control CLI and Feature Map.
  • Real-artifact evidence and independent verification.
  • Organic architecture: encode repeated lessons in types, APIs, lints, and tools.
  • Arena for alternatives, swarm for coverage, interrogation for skepticism.

Reject

  • Turning every anecdote into a permanent rule.
  • Screenshot theater or a stale verifier as “proof.”
  • PR volume as an outcome metric.

Training artifact: Build a verifier that lets a cold agent drive and prove ten user journeys, then inject defects to measure what it misses.

Kun Chen · ship / control plane

Protect human attention and authority

foundation · high

One liaison should route work to isolated workers, separate investigation from shipping, and escalate only decisions that require the captain.

Adopt

  • Firstmate → optional domain secondmates → disposable crewmates.
  • Clean worktree per task and explicit merge authority.
  • Ship versus scout task shapes.
  • Event-driven supervision and “unknown is not dead” recovery semantics.
  • Capability-, ambiguity-, cost-, and quota-aware routing.

Reject

  • A large bespoke control plane before concurrency hurts.
  • Model rankings based only on personal impressions.
  • Silent fallback that changes authority or ownership.

Training artifact: Compare single-agent, implement/review, and firstmate/two-worker modes on the same tasks; keep the hierarchy only where it cuts human interruption or lead time.

Jeffrey Emanuel · factory / work graph

Make work durable and schedulable

scale · selective

Plans, self-contained tasks, dependencies, coordination records, and procedural memory let workers remain replaceable.

Adopt

  • Detailed work packets with context, acceptance criteria, and proof.
  • Explicit dependency graph for large known programs.
  • Deterministic robot/JSON interfaces.
  • Durable coordination and session-history retrieval.
  • Convert recurring friction into tools.

Reject

  • Exhaustive plans for empirical product uncertainty.
  • Shared dirty checkout and direct-to-main as a default.
  • Agent count, commits, or lines as quality proxies.

Training artifact: Convert one 20-task migration into dependency-aware work packets and compare graph routing with FIFO/manual selection.

Steve Yegge · city / institution

Give recurring operations durable owners

operations · late

Long-lived products eventually need persistent roles, event-driven monitors, handoffs, doctrine, and feedback from production—not only task workers.

Adopt

  • Seat/session separation as a durable service-role abstraction.
  • “Crons watch, models act”: deterministic monitors wake judgment.
  • Self-authored handoffs and bounded sessions.
  • Scoped homes, explicit escalation, and blameless postmortems.
  • Feed outcomes back to the role that produced the work.

Reject

  • Red-main Land Rush outside exceptional throughput regimes.
  • Unsandboxed broad production authority.
  • Persistent-agent personhood as a correctness premise.
  • Building an agent bureaucracy that becomes the product.

Training artifact: Create one bounded event-driven operational role with scoped credentials, graceful handoff, recovery, and a postmortem loop—no artificial “company.”

Complexity budget

Never add an agent layer on faith

Every rung introduces failure modes, maintenance, cost, and coordination. Advance only when a measured requirement fails at the current rung.

00

Deterministic automation

Use normal code, scripts, static analysis, state machines, and scheduled jobs.

Advance only when judgment is genuinely required.
01

One agent with deep tools

Give one capable agent the repository, a bounded brief, and a real verification surface.

Advance when independent review or parallelism has measured value.
02

Implementer + independent reviewer

Separate creation from skepticism and use a different model family or external oracle where possible.

Advance when your attention—not worker ability—is the bottleneck.
03

Firstmate + isolated workers

One liaison dispatches clear ship work and separate scout work into clean worktrees.

Advance when ≥20 tasks have material dependency structure.
04

Dependency graph + swarm

Use durable work packets, explicit blockers, coordination, and graph-aware scheduling.

Advance when a recurring operational stream needs continuity.
05

Persistent operational roles

Assign one bounded role a charter, state, scoped authority, event triggers, handoffs, and recovery.

Stop. Add another role only after workload proves it.
Hard rule: do not build a custom orchestrator during this plan. Configure existing tools or write the thinnest adapter that makes the experiment possible. Your portfolio should prove product and engineering judgment, not recreate someone else’s 150,000-line harness.

04

Organic architecture, reliability & security

Turn recurring review judgment into code, types, APIs, module boundaries, fitness functions, and operational constraints.
90 hrsWeeks 19–28
Why this earns the time: Poteto’s strongest lesson is career-critical: the best rules are in the substrate. A senior engineer who can make agent output structurally safe scales farther than one who merely reviews more diffs.
Learn only what changes the artifact
  • Deep modules, information hiding, cohesion, coupling, reader load, and stable conceptual boundaries.
  • Type-driven domain modeling: parse at boundaries, brand primitives, and make illegal states unrepresentable.
  • Evolutionary architecture: automated fitness functions, migrations, compatibility windows, and deletion of obsolete paths.
  • Transactions, idempotency, retries, timeouts, backpressure, overload, queues, and cascading failure.
  • SLOs, error budgets, observability, least privilege, threat modeling, secrets, privacy, and cost boundaries.
  • Agent legibility: obvious project layout, canonical commands, machine-readable output, and explicit side effects.

Evidence gate

Executable verification, evals & agent safety

Build the control and observation surface that lets a cold agent prove real product behavior without you becoming the click-test bottleneck.
90 hrsWeeks 29–38
Why this earns the time: Verification is the scarce complement to cheap code generation. This phase creates a marketable asset: an executable product oracle, not a collection of prompts.
Learn only what changes the artifact

Controlled AI-engineering laboratory

Learn the four approaches by making them compete

Do not copy anyone’s mythology. Hold code, task, acceptance criteria, and environment constant; change one workflow variable; keep only what improves trusted outcomes.

Experiment 01 · Poteto

Does executable verification change autonomy?

Compare
Tests-only agent vs. the same agent with control CLI + Feature Map.
Measure
First-pass acceptance, human click minutes, missed behavior, token use.
Keep when
The verifier reduces human intervention or catches defects without unacceptable false confidence.
Experiment 02 · Chen

When does a firstmate pay for itself?

Compare
You managing two workers directly vs. one liaison managing isolated workers.
Measure
Interruptions, context switches, task lead time, duplicate work, recovery time.
Keep when
The manager cuts your attention cost while preserving exact authority and output quality.
Experiment 03 · Emanuel

When does graph scheduling beat a queue?

Compare
FIFO/manual task choice vs. dependency-aware selection on one 20-task migration.
Measure
Blocked time, critical-path duration, rework, coordination messages, graph maintenance.
Keep when
The graph shortens the real critical path enough to repay task-modeling overhead.
Experiment 04 · Yegge

Does a persistent role improve operations?

Compare
Scheduled stateless agent vs. an event-driven role with charter, history, and handoff.
Measure
Detection latency, token waste, repeated mistakes, recovery quality, operator trust.
Keep when
Continuity improves decisions—not merely personality—and authority remains bounded.
Benchmark contract
  • Fix the repository at a known commit.
  • Use explicit acceptance tests and hidden seeded defects.
  • Include 3 bugs, 3 features, 2 refactors, 2 investigations, 1 performance task, 1 security task.
  • Compare six representative tasks across all modes.
  • Use the dependency-rich migration only for graph scheduling.
  • Repeat noisy trials enough to distinguish luck from a useful pattern.
  • Do not let the author model grade its own intent-sensitive work.
  • Record failures and inconclusive runs.
  • Publish the protocol and raw scorecard with the conclusion.
Model names expire. Preserve a live registry of observed behavior by task class, and rerun the benchmark quarterly. No provider gets a permanent role because it was fashionable this month.
