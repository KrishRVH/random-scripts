The role you are training for

Judgment, not throughput

Agent runtimes already handle execution, sessions, tracing, guardrails, and orchestration. Raw implementation stops being defensible as an identity. Specification, verification, security, architecture, and accountability get more important — measured against SVPG's four risks.

Risk 01 · Value

Will anyone choose this?

Would a real user pick it over their current workaround.

Risk 02 · Usability

Can they figure it out?

Task completion without you standing there to explain it.

Risk 03 · Feasibility

Can we build & operate it?

Shippable and runnable under real constraints and failure.

Risk 04 · Viability

Does it work for the business?

Sustainable inside strategy, cost, legal, and org limits.

Why this bet · what the data says

The market already split

Fear is a bad planner; labor-market data is a better one. Four signals, checked July 2026 — re-check them quarterly and let them steer the track, not headlines.

Signal 01 · Bifurcation

Generic coding is depreciating

US software-dev postings sit roughly a third below the pre-2020 baseline (Indeed Hiring Lab) while ML/AI openings run well above it. AI-skilled engineers carry a 28–56% pay premium.

Signal 02 · Verification

Review is the new bottleneck

DORA: AI adoption raises delivery throughput and instability, together. Spec quality, review judgment, and trust systems are the scarce inputs — what Phases 4–5 train.

Signal 03 · Deployment gap

Shipping into messy reality pays

Forward-deployed engineer postings grew ~800–1,100% into 2026. The most-listed skills: customer discovery, problem decomposition, AI product judgment — Part I of this track, verbatim.

Signal 04 · Eval literacy

Evals are the hiring signal

Production teams screen for evaluation ability over framework trivia; evals, context, and agent-security work are consolidating into titled roles under the "AI product engineer" umbrella.

Roles this track maps to — use their vocabulary when you search

Product engineer · forward-deployed / applied AI engineer · AI product engineer · evals & agent-reliability roles. Postings fragment across titles and most candidates apply under the wrong one. Match the language, not just the skills.

Run the build block agent-first

Write the spec before the prompt: behavior, constraints, edge states, and how you'll verify. Delegate implementation to an agent. Review the diff like a hostile senior, then log every catch in the decision log. Diffs are cheap now; trusted change is the product. If you can't verify it, you didn't build it.

This is deliberate practice for the two skills the data says stay expensive — specification and verification — run 52 times, on real work, with a written record of what your review caught.

Part I

Human & product foundations

Weeks 1–26 · 182 hrs
01

UX, usability & accessibility

Watch someone struggle without rescuing them — then fix the workflow and prove it helped.
56 hrsWeeks 1–8
Learn
  • Mental models, mappings, affordances, signifiers, feedback, constraints
  • Information scent, recognition vs. recall, cognitive load
  • Error prevention, recovery, undo, defaults, progressive disclosure
  • Keyboard, focus, semantics, target size, contrast, assistive tech
  • Moderated usability testing & evidence-based prioritization
Produce
Per issue: evidence → violated principle → user consequence → severity → correction.
Pass condition

You distinguish an observed problem from your interpretation, prioritize by user consequence, fix the workflow, and verify the change helped.

Sources, in order
  1. Don't Make Me Think, Revisited — Krug
  2. The Design of Everyday Things — Norman
  3. NN/g — 10 Heuristics + Heuristic Evaluation
  4. WCAG 2.2 Quick Reference
  5. GOV.UK Service Manual — User Research
02

Consumer feel & visual execution

Make an interface feel intentional — and justify every decision in hierarchy, not "it looks cleaner."
56 hrsWeeks 9–16
Learn
  • Hierarchy & emphasis; density & rhythm
  • Typography & interface language
  • Responsiveness & perceived speed
  • Conventions, transitions, edge states
Teardown template
  • Intent · Attention · Interaction · State · Emotion
Produce
Preference tests only after task completion works.
Pass condition

You explain every major visual decision in terms of hierarchy, comprehension, behavior, convention, or brand.

Sources, in order
  1. Refactoring UI — Wathan & Schoger
  2. Thinking with Type, 3e — Lupton
  3. Designing Interfaces — Tidwell et al.
  4. Writing Is Designing — Metts & Welfle
  5. Mobbin + Baymard as laboratories
03

Discovery, shaping, strategy & measurement

Move from "receives requirements" to "helps decide what deserves to be built."
70 hrsWeeks 17–26
Learn
  • Outcomes, continuous customer contact, opportunity solution trees
  • Assumption identification & testing; interviewing without leading
  • Appetites, shaping, breadboards, rabbit holes, fixed-time / variable-scope
  • Hypotheses, guardrails, novelty effects, sample-ratio mismatch
  • Trustworthy numbers > easy numbers
Ask past behavior, not opinion
  • "Walk me through the last time this happened."
  • "What did that cost you? What did you try?"
Produce
Metrics: 1 primary · 1 activation · 1 retention · 2 guardrails · defined population + window.
Pass condition

You reject or reshape one of your own ideas on evidence, and can state what behavior you're changing, why it matters, and how you'll know.

Sources, in order

Part II

Technical judgment & AI-native leverage

Weeks 27–52 · 182 hrs
04

Architecture, reliability, security & evolution

Decide under uncertainty while keeping a viable path for change. A diagram without arguments isn't architecture.
70 hrsWeeks 27–36
Learn
  • Modularity, information hiding, cohesion, coupling, abstraction depth
  • Quality attributes and their conflicts
  • Data models, transactions, consistency, replication, partitioning, streams
  • Timeouts, retries, idempotency, backpressure, overload
  • Operability, security, cost, migration, org constraints
ADR template
  • Context · Decision · Alternatives · Consequences · Revisit trigger
Produce
Pass condition

You explain why the architecture fits the current product, team, scale, and failure tolerance — and name its breaking point and a credible migration path.

Sources, in order
  1. A Philosophy of Software Design, 2e — Ousterhout
  2. Fundamentals of Software Architecture, 2e — Richards & Ford
  3. Designing Data-Intensive Applications, 2e — Kleppmann & Riccomini

05

AI-native & agentic product engineering

Learn the full lifecycle, not prompt tricks. "More autonomous" is not itself a success criterion.
56 hrsWeeks 37–44
Implementation order
  • ① Deterministic code
  • ② Single model call
  • ③ Fixed workflow (route / chain / parallel)
  • ④ Bounded agent
  • ⑤ Multi-agent — most projects stop before here
Evaluation stack
  • Deterministic checks · representative cases · behavioral graders · operational measures · production feedback
Produce
Tools: narrow purpose, typed I/O, explicit side effects + authz, idempotency, machine-readable errors.
Pass condition

You show the agentic version beats the simpler baseline on a defined outcome while staying acceptable on cost, latency, reliability, and risk.

Sources, in order
  1. Anthropic — Building Effective Agents
  2. Anthropic — Effective Context Engineering
  3. OpenAI Agents SDK · Guardrails · Tracing · Evals
  4. Model Context Protocol spec
  5. NIST GenAI Profile + OWASP Agentic Top 10
06

Integrated capstone

One real product on a recurring problem. No generic task manager, habit tracker, or chat wrapper.
56 hrsWeeks 45–52
Choose a workflow where
  • People already use an awkward workaround
  • The task recurs and success is observable
  • You can reach real users
  • One bounded agentic capability may materially help
Case study arc
  • Problem → evidence → rejected ideas → chosen behavior → design → architecture → agent boundary → evaluation → measured result → remaining uncertainty
Ship all five
Include failures and reversals. A case study that hides mistakes is marketing, not judgment.
Pass condition

A senior engineer, designer, and PM can independently inspect your evidence and understand why the problem mattered, why this design and architecture, how you controlled the agent, and whether the product improved anything.

The layer skills don't cover

Skill is necessary. Position pays the rent.

In a contracting general market, hiring runs on referrals and visible proof, not application volume. Private competence protects no one. Three habits and a floor.

Habit 01 · Publish

Make the work findable

You already produce weekly artifacts. Sanitize and publish one per month — a teardown, an ADR, an eval write-up. The capstone case study is the resume.

Habit 02 · People

Two conversations a month

Talk to people already doing product-engineer, FDE, or applied-AI work. Ask what their week looks like and what they screen for. 24 conversations beat 500 applications.

Habit 03 · Domain

Pick a domain by week 26

Generic capability commoditizes first. Anchor the capstone in a domain with real constraints — money, health, logistics, compliance — where context can't be prompted away.

Position milestones
The floor, stated once: hold toward six months of expenses, and run one real interview loop every quarter or two even while employed — calibration is cheapest before you need it. Fear shrinks when the downside is funded.
What not to optimize for

Five traps that look like progress

Trap 01

Language tourism

Use the stack you ship and instrument fastest for the first 90 days.

Trap 02

Prompt collections

Prompts without datasets, graders, and failure analysis are fragile folklore.

Trap 03

Multi-agent novelty

Add agents only after simpler designs fail a measured requirement.

Trap 04

Dribbble imitation

Copying surfaces without understanding flows and users is portfolio theater.

Trap 05

Pattern collecting

A named pattern without constraints, consequences, and evidence is trivia.
